If your kid has been planning a costume since August, this is their month.

Halloween week is always fun around here. Kids show up to lessons dressed as princesses, superheroes, dinosaurs, movie characters, and things we would never have thought of on our own, and somehow there is still piano, violin, guitar, drums, and voice happening in the middle of all of it.

This year we wanted to make a bigger deal out of it, so we are turning it into a contest. Wear a costume to your lesson during Halloween Week, send us a photo, and you are in the running to win 4 tickets to The Nutcracker at Music Hall.

We also have recitals coming up on October 18, so October is shaping up to be a very CSM month: a little busy, a little chaotic, and a lot of fun.

Teacher Highlight

George

George is a long-time CSM faculty member who recently celebrated 10 years of teaching with us! Not only is he a wonderful teacher, George is also a sharp dresser. His student, Langston, decided to show up to his lesson in a bow tie, just like his teacher.

After more than 10 years at CSM, what still makes teaching enjoyable for you?

“The most enjoyable thing for me is seeing students entering the stage where they are no longer just Twinklers, but are beginning to play standard repertoire pieces, and play them well! When I trained with Shinichi Suzuki on one occasion, he had something like 50–60 string players of all ages stand up and play the same difficult solo! That’s a dream of mine, and I’m getting pretty close to seeing it happen!”

What part of teaching gives you the most satisfaction?

“My greatest enjoyment comes from moving from purely technical mechanics and begin exploring how the music is put together, letting it inform their performances.”

Most important October question: what are you dressing up as this year?

“As far as my Halloween look, I may start growing my pencil ’stache (I have to use mascara to make sure it’s black!) and come as Zorro this year!”

Parent tip

Parents, don’t become the second teacher.

If you hear mistakes during practice, know that they’re part of the learning process and don’t need correcting every time.

Your main job is encouraging and facilitating practice at home.

If a section keeps causing trouble, have your student make a note of it and bring it to the next lesson. That keeps practice at home supportive and gives the teacher a clearer picture of what the student can do independently.
